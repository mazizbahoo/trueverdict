## What it is

TrueVerdict settles everyday disagreements between two people: bills and deposits, chores, credit taken at work, a wedding, an inheritance, a friendship that stalled over a cancelled trip. If there are two sides to it, it can be ruled on.

Each person tells their side in a private conversation. A single judge reads both accounts, decides the case once, and writes each person their own letter saying honestly what they got right, what they got wrong, and how to find a way forward.

## How a case works

| Step | | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Filed** | One person opens the case. It starts as a conversation, not a form: say what happened in your own words and attach screenshots or PDFs. The judge keeps asking until the timeline, what has been tried and what you want are clear. |
| 2 | **Sealed** | You check your case as the judge will read it, correct anything that is wrong, and seal it. From then on, only the judge reads it. |
| 3 | **Served** | The other person gets a private invite link and a neutral first message that names the subject and never your position. Nobody walks in to an accusation. |
| 4 | **Answered** | They go through the same interview from their side of the wall. Neither person can read what the other wrote, before the ruling or after it. |
| 5 | **Ruled** | Both accounts are read together, agreed facts are separated from contested claims, and the case is decided a single time. |
| 6 | **Delivered** | Each person receives their own letter of 600 to 900 words, written to them and grounded in the ruling. |

## Features

- **Conversational intake.** No forms. The judge interviews you until your side is complete.
- **The privacy wall.** Your account is read by the judge and nobody else.
- **Evidence.** Up to 10 screenshots or PDFs per case.
- **One ruling per case.** Decided once and never regenerated, so both letters always agree.
- **A real decision.** Responsibility lands on one person, or is shared and split with numbers. Every finding is tied to something specific that happened.
- **Two personal letters.** Each one is honest with its reader without repeating what the other person said.
- **Neutral invitations.** The other person is invited to a question, not an accusation, and never pays to take part.
- **The Court board.** Post a disagreement publicly, let people vote and argue in the comments, and open a case when you want it settled for good.
- **Worked examples.** Read full verdicts, both letters each, on invented disputes before trying it.
