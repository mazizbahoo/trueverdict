## What it is

TrueVerdict settles everyday disagreements between two people. Each person tells their side in a private conversation. A single judge reads both accounts, decides the case once, and writes each person their own letter saying honestly what they got right, what they got wrong, and how to find a way forward.

It works for anything two people can disagree about:

`bills` `deposit` `credit` `wedding` `noise` `caregiving` `chores` `money` `inheritance` `group chat`

Flatmates splitting a heating bill, a deposit that never came back, credit taken at work, a friendship that stalled over a cancelled trip. If there are two sides to it, it can be ruled on.

## How a case works

| Step | | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Filed** | One person opens the case. It starts as a conversation, not a form: say what happened in your own words and attach screenshots or PDFs. The judge keeps asking until the timeline, what has been tried and what you want are clear. |
| 2 | **Sealed** | You check your case as the judge will read it, correct anything that is wrong, and seal it. From then on, only the judge reads it. |
| 3 | **Served** | The other person gets a private invite link and a neutral first message that names the subject and never your position. Nobody walks in to an accusation. |
| 4 | **Answered** | They go through the same interview from their side of the wall. Neither person can read what the other wrote, before the ruling or after it. |
| 5 | **Ruled** | Both accounts are read together, agreed facts are separated from contested claims, and the case is decided a single time. |
| 6 | **Delivered** | Each person receives their own letter, written to them and grounded in the ruling. |

## Who sees what

The judge is the only place your two accounts ever meet. Everything else in a case is visible to one of you, or to nobody at all.

|  | You | Them | The judge | Anyone else |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Your account and attachments | ✓ | | ✓ | |
| Their account | | ✓ | ✓ | |
| The judge's notes | | | ✓ | |
| The full ruling | | | ✓ | |
| Your letter | ✓ | | | |
| Their letter | | ✓ | | |

Cases are never published. There is no public case page and no share button.

## The letter

- **600 to 900 words**, written to you by name.
- **A real decision.** Responsibility lands on one person, or it is shared and split with numbers. It never stops at "you were both a bit wrong".
- **Specific.** Every finding is tied to something that actually happened.
- **Honest without exposing.** Your letter can tell you where you went wrong without quoting anything the other person said.
- **Consistent.** Both letters come from the same single ruling, so they never contradict each other.
- **Forward looking.** Each letter ends with how to move on, and where the accounts leave something unresolved, it says what would change the outcome.

Want to see one first? The site has worked examples on invented disputes, with both letters in full for each.

## The Court board

Not everything needs a ruling.

- **Post** a disagreement publicly with a short label like `bills` or `chores`.
- **Vote** on who is right and **argue it out** in the comments.
- **Browse** by topic, **search** posts, and visit people's profiles.
- **Report** posts that break the rules.
- **Escalate** to a private case whenever you want it settled for good.

Posting, voting and commenting are free on every plan.

## FAQ

**Can an AI really understand my situation?**
It works from what you tell it, and it asks. The interview keeps going until the timeline, what has been tried and what you actually want are clear.

**Will the other person see what I wrote?**
No. Their letter is written without your account, so it has nothing of yours to quote.

**What if they won't take part?**
The invitation names the subject and never your position, so it reads as a question rather than an accusation. It costs them nothing.

**Is the verdict legally binding?**
No. It is a considered, direct opinion. It is not legal advice and not a substitute for a lawyer, therapist or mediator.
